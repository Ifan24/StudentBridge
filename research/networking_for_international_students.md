# Research Notes: Networking for International Students

These notes are for understanding the topic and collecting evidence. They are not a completed assessment response.

## Working definition

For international students in Australia, networking can mean four connected things:

1. Social networking: making friends, reducing isolation and building a local support circle.
2. Academic networking: connecting with classmates, study groups, student clubs and peer mentors.
3. Professional networking: meeting employers, alumni, industry mentors and career advisers.
4. Community networking: finding local events, volunteering, government support hubs and multicultural groups.

## Why this topic matters

Networking is important because international students are often building a new life and career pathway at the same time. A useful networking system can help them:

- settle into Australia faster
- make friends and practise English
- understand Australian workplace expectations
- find volunteer, internship and industry experience opportunities
- discover support services before problems become serious
- build confidence for casual work and graduate employment

## Evidence from current sources

### 1. The international student market is large and diverse

The Australian Department of Education reported that 551,717 international students studied in Australia in January 2026. The same monthly summary says 58% of students were from China, India, Nepal, Vietnam and Bangladesh.

Research implication:

- Networking features should support different cultures, languages, study levels and city locations.
- A one-size-fits-all social app is unlikely to be enough.

Source:

- Australian Department of Education, International student monthly summary and data tables: https://www.education.gov.au/international-education-data-and-research/international-student-monthly-summary-and-data-tables

### 2. Students rate Australia positively, but career/work experience is weaker

QILT's 2024 international student findings show high overall living satisfaction, with 92.5% of undergraduate international students and 92.0% of postgraduate coursework students rating their overall living experience positively. However, work experience in the field of study was the lowest-rated living-experience item.

Research implication:

- Networking should not only focus on friendship. It should also connect students to career events, industry projects, mentors, alumni and employers.
- A feature that links students to field-related experience could solve a real pain point.

Source:

- QILT, 2024 Student Experience Survey - International Key Findings: https://qilt.edu.au/docs/default-source/default-document-library/2024-ses-international-key-findings.pdf

### 3. Support services exist, but many students do not use them

Study Australia's 2024 Student Survey found that only 17% of surveyed international students had used a local state or territory government support service or study hub. However, students who did use these services reported 92% satisfaction.

Research implication:

- The issue may be awareness and access, not only lack of services.
- A networking app could act as a discovery bridge between students and official support hubs, city events, legal help, accommodation help and employment help.

Source:

- Study Australia, You spoke, we listened: results from our 2024 Student Survey: https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/life-in-australia/student-support-services/international-student-sentiment-survey

### 4. Industry experience is a networking pathway

The Study Australia Industry Experience Program connects domestic and international students with employers through real industry projects. Study Australia says the program helps students build networks and boost employability. The page also reports that SAIEP has engaged nearly 8000 students from 55 education providers, working with over 1000 Australian and international employers.

Research implication:

- International students value structured, low-risk ways to meet employers.
- Networking features could include industry project matching, employer Q&A sessions, virtual project groups, and alumni/employer mentoring.

Source:

- Study Australia Industry Experience Program: https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/work-in-australia/getting-work-and-industry-experience/study-australia-industry-experience-program-saiep

### 5. Employers value international students' global perspective

Study Australia's employer guide says international students can bring diverse perspectives, adaptability, global mindset, language skills, cross-cultural competencies and international networks.

Research implication:

- Networking tools should help students present their strengths clearly, not only search for jobs.
- Useful features could include a profile builder, networking pitch prompts, resume workshops, and employer-facing student talent showcases.

Source:

- Study Australia, What employers are looking for: https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/tools-and-resources/tips-and-advice-for-students/what-employers-are-looking-for

### 6. Local hubs and clubs are practical networking channels

Study Melbourne says its hub hosts free activities and events for international students, including resume workshops and social events. It also says students can use societies, clubs, sport and interest groups to meet people and build skills.

Research implication:

- A networking app should integrate local events, campus clubs, city study hubs and professional workshops.
- Events should be filterable by city, interest, career field, language and cost.

Source:

- Study Melbourne, Groups and communities: https://studymelbourne.vic.gov.au/living-here/social-life/groups-and-communities

### 7. Volunteer and industry experience are official pathways

Study Australia identifies internships, SAIEP, volunteering and career tools as ways to complement study and gain valuable experience.

Research implication:

- Networking features should include volunteering and internships, not only paid work.
- Volunteer experience can help students make local contacts, practise English and build Australian references.

Source:

- Study Australia, Volunteer and industry experience: https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/work-in-australia/getting-work-and-industry-experience.html

## Possible app feature ideas

These are not final recommendations, just ideas to test:

- City-based networking feed for international student events.
- Filters for career area, language, location, free/paid event, online/in-person.
- Peer buddy matching for new arrivals.
- Alumni mentor matching by course area and city.
- Verified employer networking events.
- Industry project opportunities similar to SAIEP.
- Volunteering directory.
- Student club and society directory.
- Resume and LinkedIn workshop calendar.
- Australian workplace culture mini-guides.
- "Ask a local student" or "Ask an alumni mentor" feature.
- Safety controls: verified profiles, event moderation, report/block functions.
- Privacy controls: show first name only, hide exact address, opt-in messaging.

## Possible business/sponsor angle

Networking can create value for sponsors because:

- education providers want better student engagement and retention
- employers want access to diverse graduate talent
- study hubs want students to use support services
- local businesses want student foot traffic
- professional associations want future members
- local councils want international students to participate in community life

Possible sponsors:

- universities and colleges
- student accommodation providers
- local councils and study hubs
- employers and recruiters
- professional bodies
- coworking spaces
- banks, insurers and migration/legal information providers
- cafes and event venues near campuses

## Useful survey or interview questions

For international students:

1. What kind of networking do you need most: friends, career, industry, study support, or local community?
2. What makes it difficult to meet local students or professionals?
3. Which channels do you currently use: university clubs, LinkedIn, WeChat, WhatsApp, Meetup, Eventbrite, study hubs, friends?
4. Would you use an app that lists verified networking events and mentors?
5. What safety or privacy features would make you trust the app?
6. Which networking events would you attend: career workshops, employer talks, social events, volunteering, sport, language exchange?

For sponsors/employers:

1. Would you pay to promote events or opportunities to international students?
2. What outcome would matter most: registrations, attendance, applications, store visits, or brand awareness?
3. What student information would you need, and what should remain private?
4. Would verified student profiles increase your trust?

## How this could fit Module 3

Networking can be used as one app feature category inside the broader international student app idea.

It connects to the assessment questions like this:

- Main users: international students who need friends, career contacts and local support.
- User requirements: safe events, trusted groups, career contacts, language support and mentor access.
- App features: event directory, mentor matching, industry projects, student clubs and verified employer sessions.
- Sponsors: education providers, employers, study hubs, councils and professional bodies.
- Revenue: promoted events, sponsor listings, employer packages, education-provider partnerships.
- Popularity: networking features can drive repeat use because students keep looking for new friends, events and career opportunities after arrival.
